Maths notes that help you solve questions

Turn a worked example into a compact method sheet, then use a simple error log to guide your next practice question.

In this guide
  1. Write the action beside the working
  2. Include a check you can repeat
  3. Record the mistake, not the whole lesson
  4. Turn the notes into a question

Maths notes are most useful when they help you take the next step in a question. A page full of copied solutions can be difficult to use if it does not explain why each line follows from the last.

Try making a short method sheet for one skill. Include a worked example, the reason for each step, a check and one mistake to watch for. Leave space for a question you will attempt without looking.

Write the action beside the working

Consider this equation:

3(2x−5)=9

There are two sensible routes. You could expand the bracket first. Here, dividing both sides by 3 gives a shorter first line:

  1. Divide both sides by 3. The whole bracket is multiplied by 3, so undo that multiplication: 2x − 5 = 3.
  2. Add 5 to both sides. Undo the subtraction: 2x = 8.
  3. Divide both sides by 2. Undo the multiplication by 2: x = 4.

The important note is not just “divide, add, divide”. Write the principle: apply the same operation to both sides so the equality is preserved. That instruction also works when the numbers change.

Include a check you can repeat

Substitute 4 into the original left-hand side:

3(2×4−5)=3×3=9

The result matches the right-hand side, so the solution checks. Keep this substitution beside the method. It gives you something specific to do when you are uncertain about a later answer.

You could also note the expansion route: 6x − 15 = 9, then 6x = 24 and x = 4. Comparing the two routes shows that a valid method can have more than one starting point.

Record the mistake, not the whole lesson

A compact error log can sit underneath your notes. Describe what went wrong precisely enough that you can act on it.

Example entries for an algebra error log
What happenedWhat to check next timePractice task
I expanded 3(2x − 5) as 6x − 5.Multiply every term inside the bracket by 3.Expand three brackets before solving another equation.
I changed only one side of the equation.Write the operation on both sides.Solve two equations with one operation per line.

You do not need an entry for every slip. Choose the mistakes that recur or reveal a gap in your method.

Turn the notes into a question

Close the method sheet and try 4(3x − 2) = 28. Write each operation on its own line and check your result in the original equation.

Check your practice answer

Divide by 4 to get 3x − 2 = 7. Add 2 to get 3x = 9, then divide by 3: x = 3. Substitution gives 4(9 − 2) = 28.

If you need to reopen the notes, identify the exact step that required help. Practise that step, then try a different question later. A finished set of notes should lead you back to working independently, with a clearer method and a check you understand.
