Does this sound familiar?

Most students who struggle with maths aren't bad at it. Usually one missing step or an earlier idea never quite clicked.

Before

  • Follows the lesson in class, then can't start the homework alone
  • Copies out the right answer after a mistake, then makes it again
  • Revises for hours without knowing what actually needs work
  • Loses marks on working, even when the answer is right

With lessons

  • We find the exact step that's missing and fix it, without rushing
  • Each mistake becomes a related question they can now answer
  • Clear feedback on what's secure and what to practise next
  • Written solutions set out the way examiners award marks

How lessons work

A clear routine, so your child knows what to expect and you can see the progress.

  1. Find the gap

    I ask your child to explain a step or choose a method, which shows what a finished page of working can hide.

  2. Explain and model

    A clear explanation and a worked example, linked to their course and exam board's notation.

  3. Practise with purpose

    Questions that build in difficulty, from getting the method secure to tackling unfamiliar problems.

  4. Feedback and next steps

    What's understood, what needs more care, and what to practise before the next lesson.

Questions parents ask

How much do lessons cost?

One-to-one lessons are £60 per hour. Group classes are £30 per student per hour.

Which courses and exam boards do you teach?

GCSE Maths, GCSE Further Maths, A-level Maths and A-level Further Maths, across AQA, Edexcel, OCR and OCR MEI. My question packs also cover IB Maths and OCR Additional Maths.

Are lessons online or in person?

Both. Online lessons run on Zoom anywhere in the UK. In-person lessons are available in Notting Hill, subject to availability.

What does my child need for online lessons?

A laptop or tablet with Zoom, plus pen and paper. We use a shared digital whiteboard, and your child can write on it with a tablet if they have one, but it isn't essential.

What's the difference between one-to-one and group classes?

One-to-one lessons are planned entirely around your child. Group classes follow a set routine for a small group: learn the method, try it, then review the working together.
