Build a maths revision plan around the questions you struggle with

A practical way to choose revision topics, organise a manageable week and adjust the plan using your own question attempts.

In this guide
  1. Choose topics from evidence
  2. Build a modest week
  3. Give each session a beginning and an end
  4. Make room to revisit and adjust

A useful maths revision plan tells you what to do when you sit down. “Revise algebra” leaves a lot to decide. “Try six equations with brackets, check the answers and redo the ones I missed” gives you a clear starting point and a way to judge the session.

You do not need a timetable that fills every spare hour. Start with a small plan you can fit around lessons, homework and other commitments. Then change it using the questions you actually attempt.

Choose topics from evidence

Take a recent homework, test or short set of mixed questions. Make a list of the topics that caused trouble. Be specific: “subtracting algebraic fractions” is more useful than “fractions”, and “choosing the right trigonometric ratio” is more useful than “geometry”.

Give each topic a simple working label:

  • Start: I cannot yet choose a method without help.
  • Practise: I know the method but make mistakes or need prompts.
  • Check: I can answer questions independently and want to revisit the topic later.

These labels describe your current attempts, not your ability. Pick two topics to work on this week. A long list can stay in your notebook; it does not all need to go into the timetable.

Build a modest week

Here is one possible week for a student working on equations with brackets and right-angled trigonometry. The timings are suggestions. Shorten a session if necessary, while keeping a clear task.

Example revision week: 100 minutes in total
WhenTimeSpecific task
Monday25 minutesStudy one equations example, then attempt six questions independently.
Wednesday25 minutesRedo Monday’s missed questions, then try a short mixed algebra set.
Friday20 minutesLabel opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse on three diagrams; solve three trigonometry questions.
Sunday30 minutesTry questions from both topics without notes, check them and choose next week’s priorities.

Attach sessions to times that are realistic for you. If Tuesday already contains a long homework assignment, moving revision to another day may make the plan easier to follow.

Give each session a beginning and an end

At the start, write the precise skill you are practising. If you need a reminder, look at one worked example and explain each step. Then close it and attempt your own questions.

Allow time for checking. Put a small mark beside any answer that used a hint, even if the final answer is correct. That question is a useful candidate for another independent attempt.

Finish by writing one sentence: “Next time, I need to check the sign when I expand a negative bracket.” This makes the next session easier to start.

Make room to revisit and adjust

Keep at least one session for earlier questions. An answer you can reproduce immediately after reading a solution may still need another attempt on a different day.

If a topic remains difficult, narrow the task. Instead of another full set of trigonometry problems, practise identifying the sides first. If it feels secure, add a question with different wording or combine it with another topic.

A missed session does not require rewriting the whole week. Move the most useful task to your next available slot. Keep the plan small enough to use, and let your work decide what belongs in it.
